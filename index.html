About Me

Obligate Bipedalism: The ability to walk habitually upright on two feet, freeing the hands for tool use and carrying objects. This is supported by a unique skeletal structure (e.g., S-shaped spine, bowl-shaped pelvis, arch in the foot). Large and Complex Brain: The human brain is disproportionately large relative to body size, with a highly developed cerebral cortex, enabling advanced cognitive functions. Opposable Thumbs and Fine Motor Skills: Highly precise manipulation skills due to the structure of the hand and opposable thumbs, which is essential for making and using complex tools. Omnivorous Diet: The ability to digest both plant and animal matter, which has contributed to our adaptability across diverse environments. Extended Childhood: Humans have an unusually long period of dependency and development, allowing more time for learning complex cultural and social skills. Distinctive Skull Features: A high, rounded braincase, a flat and near-vertical forehead, minimal or no brow ridges, and a prominent chin on the lower jaw.
